# OpenBGA Fresh CoA LIMS product

OpenBGA Fresh CoA LIMS is part of [OpenBGA](https://github.com/openbga). The repository is [openbga/fresh-coa-lims](https://github.com/openbga/fresh-coa-lims).

Are you looking for support? Contact [bgasoft.com](https://bgasoft.com).

OpenBGA Fresh CoA LIMS supports manufacturing quality-control laboratories in pharma/biotech, dietary supplements, food, chemical/industrial and medical devices in **one system**. Segment and destination-market obligations are controlled configuration, not separate products. One organization/site per deployment and online staff access are initial assumptions; neither a customer portal nor shared tenancy is part of this foundation.

The [regulatory matrix](regulations.md) is the authority for researched applicability, sources, jurisdiction and caveats, with a baseline of 2026-11-10. No environmental laboratory rule is inherited merely because code is reusable.

The platform and bounded native QC modules are under development. This is not a completed MVP or authorization for regulated production. The product supports a laboratory's quality system; it does not certify compliance with Part 11, cGMP, QMSR or ISO. Identity proofing, intended-use validation, qualified personnel, approved procedures and recovery remain deployment responsibilities. Laboratory approval, QC disposition and manufacturer release/QP certification are distinct decisions.

## Users and authority

| Role | Responsibility | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Analyst | Receive lots/samples, record observations and batch QC | Cannot review their own work |
| Reviewer | Review original records and results with PIN signature | Must be independent of authorship; return/correction flows retain history |
| Quality | Approve configuration, keep the supplier register and make QC disposition decisions | Cannot bypass missing/failing requirements or self-review |
| Admin | Read every laboratory area; manage named users and roles; control the sign-in announcement | No scientific mutation or signing without its designated lab role; no self-granted additional roles or removal of the last active admin |
| Auditor | Read records and inspect audit history | No scientific mutation |

The initial roles map to explicit Django groups. Later receiving, supervisor and metrology responsibilities may be split without weakening independence. Qualifications belong to the relevant method/activity, not merely to group membership. Account ownership must remain individual; shared signers are prohibited. Superusers receive no implicit laboratory or web account-management role. Accounts without a recognized role reach an access-guidance page instead of a permission loop.

Account administrators can create users, update identity, assign/revoke roles, deactivate/reactivate, clear PIN lockout, require PIN re-enrollment and force password replacement. Every administrative web change requires a reason and a fresh username/PIN signature bound to the resulting account revision. Initial and optional recovery passwords are entered privately and must be replaced before workspace access; no email delivery is assumed. Changes invalidate existing target sessions. Self-service password replacement requires the current password and a reason, with an audit event but no PIN, so credential recovery remains possible. The audited operator commands support bootstrap and controlled recovery; they are a separate trusted channel.

Account administrators also control one sign-in announcement: a notice shown on the sign-in page to anyone, before authentication. They write, publish and withdraw it with a reason and signature, and every version stays in the audit trail. It exists for operational notices and, on a synthetic demonstration instance, for listing shared demonstration accounts. Such accounts are not individual signers; an instance that publishes them holds demonstration data only, and must never list an account that can change the announcement. Accounts the demonstration seed publishes cannot change their own password or PIN.

Accepted limitation: administrators are trusted to perform account administration. An admin who sets an initial or temporary password and requires PIN re-enrollment could act as that account. The application does not prevent this; laboratory SOPs and review of the audit trail (including `account.created`, `account.reset_password` and `account.reenroll_pin`) are the mitigations.

## Journeys

1. Receive an identified material/manufacturing lot in quarantine with its receipt details: the supplier or that it was made on site, the supplier's and manufacturer's lot numbers, the delivery document, the date, the quantity, the containers and what the examination on receipt found. Attach the approved effective rule revision, assign approved specifications and a sampling plan, and record sampling provenance.
2. Assign tests from the frozen requirements; record numeric, count or qualitative observations, or calculate a numeric result from raw inputs with the method's approved formula; record replicates and required batch QC with units, limits, the instruments and reagent or standard lots used, raw-data references or attached raw-data files, and attribution. Preserve applied rule/specification/method revisions.
3. Have a different qualified person inspect source data and audit history, then re-enter username and signing PIN to review the exact revision.
4. Have an authorized quality person evaluate completeness, failures and outstanding holds and sign the QC disposition. A laboratory decision never silently implies manufacturer batch release.
5. Issue a versioned CoA from approved evidence; amendment preserves the original and states the reason. Native issuance stores the evidence snapshot and PDF; later downloads return the issued bytes.

A failure creates a visible hold/investigation path. Retesting, resampling or invalidation must never erase the original result or allow testing into compliance. Stability protocols generate pull records with acceptance and evidence links. Released lot evidence is immutable, so new stability testing on a released lot currently requires controlled external evidence. Recording or correcting an observation or a batch QC result requires a current approved analyst qualification for the method, names the instruments and the reagent or standard lots used, and is refused when one of them is not fit for use. The evidence relied on is kept with the result. The separate resource-use page still records use against a sample outside native testing.

## In-app page documentation

Every staff page offers documentation from an icon at the top-right of the top bar. It opens a resizable right-side panel (a full-width overlay on narrow screens), follows navigation, and remembers its open/closed state and width in the browser. Each entry explains purpose, permissions, layout and every tab, action and field, naming `model.field` where applicable. It also explains hidden filters, defaults, permission conditions, transitions, validations and signature/audit effects. Documentation is derived from source, labels unverified policy as an assumption, and changes with the page. Automated coverage and reference checks are part of the release gate; prose accuracy remains the author’s responsibility.

## Implemented scope, acceptance and later work

| Stage | Scope | Acceptance boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Implemented modules | Shared rules/signatures/audit; versioned catalog and sampling; supplier register and lot receipt details; numeric, count, qualitative and calculated testing/replicates/QC/OOS with raw-data files; equipment, reagent and analyst checks at result entry; stability/retention/holds; native PDF CoA | Covered by PostgreSQL tests and native end-to-end browser checks; intended-use and operational acceptance remain |
| Remaining MVP integration/policy | Results from supplier certificates and contract laboratories, supplier qualification, destination/customer applicability, supported assay coverage and operational validation | Complete accepted/failed/corrected/amended journeys and traceability to every applicable requirement |
| Later capability | Advanced SPC/OOT, automated instrument interfaces, broader inventory/ELN/CAPA, specialized assays and environmental monitoring | Separate scoped acceptance and applicability review |

The public [roadmap](https://openbga.github.io/fresh-coa-lims/roadmap.html) groups the remaining and later work into capability areas, without dates. Its source is `website/roadmap.html`.

The native analytical contract supports three result types, each set on the approved specification line. **Numeric** results and **counts** (whole numbers of zero or more) are compared with a lower limit, an upper limit or both; each limit either includes or excludes its boundary. A declared reporting precision rounds the replicate mean half-up before comparison; `reporting_decimals=null` means exact comparison without rounding. Submission rejects a nonterminating mean or an exact mean requiring more than eight decimal places rather than silently rounding it. Individual replicate limits are checked when configured. A numeric or count result may be recorded as **less than a value** (for example `<10`). It is stored and reported as recorded and never becomes zero or a number. It conforms only when the line has no lower limit and the stated value does not exceed the upper limit; otherwise it cannot be shown to conform and is treated as not conforming. When any replicate is a less-than result no mean is calculated and every replicate must conform on its own. **Qualitative** results are chosen from a controlled pair (conforms or does not conform; absent or present) against the approved expected outcome and criterion, and each records what was observed. Every replicate must show the expected outcome. The less-than comparison and the replicate rules are project design defaults, not cited requirements (see REG-RT02 and REG-RT07 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md)); a quality owner approves them for the intended use. Greater-than results, method-specific rounding conventions and batch QC checks other than directly entered numeric values with inclusive limits are not supported. A qualitative line approved before expected outcomes existed is held until a new specification version states one. The software does not interpret acceptance criteria written as powers of ten; the approved line states the explicit maximum. No preset may silently bypass these boundaries.

A method version may define one **calculation**: a formula over 1 to 12 named inputs with units, using addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and parentheses, with the unit of the calculated value and the number of decimals it is rounded to. It is approved and frozen with the method version. For such a method the analyst enters the inputs instead of a value. The system calculates the replicate value, rounds it half-up to the approved decimals and stores the inputs as entered, the formula and the method version with the observation. Submission and review calculate again from the stored inputs and refuse a difference. A specification line that uses a calculated method must be numeric in the calculated unit; otherwise the requirement is held. A calculated result cannot be reported as less than a value, and batch QC checks are entered directly. Arithmetic carries 50 significant digits before rounding; rounding of intermediate values, significant figures and unit conversion are not applied. These are project design defaults, not cited requirements, and a quality owner approves each formula for its intended use.

A method version may also require that every result and batch QC entry records at least one instrument, at least one reagent or standard lot, or both. Whatever is selected is checked when the entry is recorded: equipment must be in service with an independently accepted, unexpired calibration and, where required, current qualification evidence; a reagent or standard lot and its source lots must be released and unexpired. The analyst needs a current approved qualification for the method, for batch QC as well. A refused entry stores nothing and the refusal is audited. The method does not say which instrument or lot is suitable; that remains the analyst's and the reviewer's judgement. After a failed calibration the equipment page lists the tests and QC batches that used the equipment since its last accepted passing calibration.

**Raw-data files** can be attached to an open test by an analyst. Each file is stored in PostgreSQL exactly as received with its SHA-256 checksum, can never be changed or deleted, and is returned unchanged on download after the checksum is verified. A file is limited to 10 MiB by default (`RAW_DATA_MAX_BYTES`) and a test to 20 files; a deployment can also cap all files together (`RAW_DATA_TOTAL_MAX_BYTES`). Files are not opened, converted or scanned by the software, and they are not part of the certificate.

**Receipt details** are part of receiving a lot: a lot is received with them or not at all. They state where the lot came from (a supplier from the register, or made on site), the supplier's and the manufacturer's lot numbers when known, the manufacturer when it is not the supplier, the delivery or batch document, the date received, the quantity with its unit, the number and kind of containers, and what the examination on receipt found. The details are never edited: while the lot is in quarantine an analyst corrects them by adding a record that states its reason and links to the one it replaces. A correction advances the lot's revision, so a disposition page opened before it must be reviewed again. Whoever recorded or corrected a lot's receipt details cannot sign its disposition. Quality keeps the **supplier register**, which records who a supplier is. It does not qualify a supplier, and naming a supplier does not reduce testing. A discrepancy found on receipt is recorded and shown; it does not place a hold by itself. Lots received before receipt details were kept have none until an analyst records them. What the examination on receipt must cover and how suppliers are approved are decided by the laboratory's own procedures; the fields are project design defaults derived from REG-P17, REG-P28 and REG-S09 in the [regulatory matrix](regulations.md).

Once native tests exist, the lot requirements cannot be reassigned; controlled test/requirement supersession is not implemented. New QC batches offer only currently effective approved method revisions. Tests frozen to an older revision cannot use a newer-method batch, and creating a new batch for their old revision is intentionally unavailable.

Production QC disposition requires a native assignment and passing analytical/hold checks. The original manual-limit result path is DEBUG-only and unavailable once a lot has native assignments; mixed evidence blocks disposition. Released lots cannot accept new scientific results. Stability studies on those lots therefore retain external controlled test evidence rather than reopening the released lot.

Five editable segment presets are examples requiring quality-owner review. They are not authoritative legal policies, complete panels or production defaults. Universal audit, signature linkage and no-self-review cannot be disabled by a preset. Unresolved applicability must hold decisions; a generic preset cannot authorize a specialized assay. Deferred native functionality requires a controlled external process before the corresponding regulated work is supported.
