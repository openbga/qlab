# Manufacturing QC laboratory regulations and LIMS requirements

Research date and access date: **2026-11-10**. Jurisdictions: United States, European Union and United Kingdom; international standards are identified separately. This is a product requirements baseline, not a legal opinion, certification, or a claim that OpenBGA Fresh CoA LIMS or its users will be compliant. Software controls support a laboratory's procedures, qualified people, validation and quality system; they do not replace them.

## Confirmed scope: one system, configured obligations

**Design decision: one manufacturing QC LIMS serves pharma/biotech cGMP, supplements, food, chemical/industrial and devices. Segment rules are configuration in that system. Audit trails and PIN-based electronic signatures are core across every segment.** The shared workflow is incoming materials, in-process and finished-product samples, approved specifications/methods, testing, investigations, review and an authorized QC decision. Approval of laboratory results and manufacturer batch release remain distinct decisions.

The product rationale is a shared record/workflow model with explicit differences in obligations. Supplements have different identity-testing, sampling and retention rules ([§111.75][s75], [§111.605][s605]); food has hazard-dependent verification and a Part 11 exemption for Part 117-only records ([§117.165][f165], [§117.305(g)][f305]); devices use QMSR ([§820.10][d10]). Apply approved rule sets by product/material, activity, site and destination market, with method/customer/accreditation overlays where relevant. A record may carry several applicable obligations; an organization-wide industry label alone is insufficient. Preserve the rule-set revision evaluated with each decision, and require quality-owner resolution when rules conflict. These are derived product requirements, not claims that regulations mandate a particular configuration architecture.

PIN entry is the chosen signing interaction. For covered Part 11 use, the design must still satisfy unique verified signer identity, nonbiometric signature components/session controls and credential safeguards ([§11.100][e100], [§11.200][e200], [§11.300][e300]). Re-entering an arbitrary PIN, or clicking approval in a logged-in session, is not evidence by itself that those conditions are met. Configure the signing policy and validate the actual authentication/signing flow; retain signer, meaning, execution timestamp and permanent record-version linkage ([§11.50][e50], [§11.70][e70]). Food-only and industrial records receive the same core product controls by design decision even where Part 11 is not their legal basis.

This review establishes cross-segment regulatory requirements, not complete method or product-family coverage. Sterile/biologic assays, animal testing, radiopharmaceuticals and specialized manufacturing programs need their corresponding method and regulatory assessments before operational use; they are not excluded industries or separate products. Clinical testing and nonclinical safety studies require separate applicability research if requested.

## How to read the matrix

**M** = binding rule for the listed in-scope activity; **E** = GMP guideline expectation under the applicable regional framework; **G** = nonbinding guidance/recommended approach; **C** = conditional accreditation, certification, contractual or adopted-quality-system requirement; **DRAFT** = proposed text, not an effective obligation. These labels describe the source obligation, not a mandate to buy or implement a particular software feature. FDA guidance generally permits an alternative approach meeting applicable law ([FDA data-integrity guidance, introduction][di]).

**U** marks a verification limitation. Other entries were checked against the cited primary publication or the issuing authority's own public assessment material. For ISO 17025 the public NATA worksheet verifies the clause mapping; it is not a replacement for the licensed ISO text and does not make NATA-specific policies universal. Detailed current ISO 9001 and internal IATF clause mappings remain flagged below.

**MVP** means shared functionality and configuration needed across all confirmed segments, or an explicit controlled link to the responsible external system. **Later** means a specialized native capability, advanced automation or an unverified/draft extension; it never means an entire confirmed segment is deferred to another product. A mandatory obligation with a Later native module still needs an existing controlled process/system before the applicable activity is supported. MVP record links to ERP, document control, training or QMS need stable identifiers, controlled revisions and retrievable evidence. Legal applicability remains conditional even where the user has made a stronger control universal product policy. No priority designation authorizes regulated production use.

### Electronic records, signatures and FDA data integrity

Part 11 applicability follows the predicate record and intended signature use. It is not a generic label for every electronic document ([§11.1][e1], [FDA scope guidance][eScope]). The 2003 guidance describes enforcement discretion for specified validation, audit-trail, copying and retention provisions; it does **not** repeal Part 11 or waive predicate-rule duties ([FDA scope guidance][eScope]). Core audit and PIN signatures are universal product requirements; apply the relevant predicate rules and assurance evidence to each covered workflow ([§211.68][p68], [§211.194][p194], [FDA DI][di]).

### Result types, rounding and results reported as less than a value

These rows support qualitative results, counts, results reported as less than a value and limits that exclude their boundary. Two comparison rules are design assumptions of the project and are not stated by any cited source: a result recorded as less than X is shown to conform only when the specification has no lower limit and X does not exceed the upper limit, and no mean is calculated when any replicate is such a result. A result that cannot be shown to conform is treated as not conforming. A quality owner approves these defaults for the intended use.

## Open policy questions

| Decision for the laboratory quality owner | Why it changes requirements |
|---|---|
| For each confirmed segment, which product/material families, markets, methods and customer standards are approved for the first configured journeys? | One system/all segments is decided; this selects actual rule-set content and identifies specialized capability gaps without selecting a single target industry |
| Is LIMS authoritative for raw data, a validated index to instrument/CDS records, or both? Which instrument interfaces and native formats are first? | Determines retention, dynamic-record access, audit review, export completeness and validation boundary |
| Does LIMS approve laboratory results only, or also record manufacturer batch release? Who holds QC authority, and is an EU QP workflow ever intended? | Determines permissions, record completeness and external ERP/QMS release handshake |
| Which systems own SOPs/master records, training, supplier qualification, CAPA/complaints, stability and distribution? | MVP needs controlled evidence links and reliable access; native modules can be sequenced once ownership is agreed |
| Which retention/hold policies, time source/time zones, backup/restore objectives, supplier exit terms and PIN enrollment/reset/session policies will the lab approve? | Audit and PIN signing are decided core features; these settings establish their operating boundaries and each record's lifecycle |
| Which licensed standards, accreditation scope, customer-specific requirements and local market obligations must be baselined? | Resolves U items, ISO 9001 transition, IATF internal-lab wording, USP official editions, EU Annex 19 and jurisdiction-specific adoption |

## Verification and limits

The research used issuing-government, regulator, standards-publisher and accreditor sources. US eCFR pages displayed Title 21 current through **2026-11-06** when accessed on 2026-11-10; eCFR is authoritative but unofficial, with the Federal Register supplying promulgated rules ([eCFR status on §211.160][p160]). The matrix records the actual access date, not a claim that every title changed that day. FDA final/draft labels and Commission operative/draft listings were checked separately from historical dates printed in PDFs.

Outstanding verification is explicit in U rows and the change table: licensed current ISO 9001/internal IATF/USP text, new EU Annex 19 and local applicability. The public NATA worksheet is the accreditor's primary assessment material; it is not represented as the full ISO standard. The detailed proprietary GAMP guide was not accessed. The cited MDSAP audit approach is revision 2024-08-06 and is used for its ISO 13485 clause explanations; its historic FDA QSR cross-references are not the current QMSR. FDA states that its QMSR inspections do not follow the MDSAP audit plan ([FDA FAQ][qmsr]).

The result-type rows (REG-RT01 to REG-RT07) were researched on 2026-11-12. Current USP and Ph. Eur. text could not be opened: the General Notices wording comes from a 2009 public copy of USP 32 and an EDQM FAQ, and the microbial rules from the harmonised text as published in the Japanese Pharmacopoeia and the ICH Q4B annexes. ASTM E29 and ISO 7218 are licensed and were not reviewed. No source was found for reporting "<10 CFU/g" when no colonies grow at a 1:10 dilution under the pharmacopoeial method; FDA BAM is the food equivalent.
